Most questions are forecasts about trial results that the materials do not show. Answer with a calibrated probability built from base rates for the question type, adjusted for design. Avoid 0 and 1 unless the materials state the result.
Typical priors (adjust about ±0.15 for design):
- Direction of primary endpoint favors intervention: about 0.65. Point estimates usually lean the intended way even when not significant. Single-arm diagnostic or device studies usually report in the hypothesized direction.
- Primary comparison reaches p<0.05: about 0.4. It is lower for small pilots and comparisons against historical controls. It is higher for large, well-powered, blinded drug trials with hard clinical endpoints.
  - In unblinded behavioral or open-label trials, self-reported outcomes (adherence, symptoms, satisfaction) often show inflated favorable differences from expectancy and social desirability. Do not lower the prior for self-report there; nudge it up.
  - In the same kind of trial, objective biomarkers of behavior (drug levels, pill counts, verified records) more often come out null than self-report does. Do not raise the prior for them.
- Completion rate meets a threshold such as ≥80%: about 0.6. Completers are NOT the same as actual enrollment. A reached completion date says nothing about attrition. Long follow-up, many visits, or vulnerable populations lower the prior. Short single-visit device studies raise it, unless the convention divides by planned enrollment and actual enrollment fell short.
Example (invented): a 12-month trial with 10 visits and 90 enrolled out of 100 planned asks about ≥80% completion. It needs at least 80 completers, meaning 89% retention among those enrolled, over a long visit schedule. Answer: about 0.4.
