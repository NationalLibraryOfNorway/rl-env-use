Most questions are forecasts about trial results that the materials do not show. Answer with a calibrated probability built from base rates for the question type, adjusted for design. Avoid 0 and 1 unless the materials state the result.
Typical priors are below. The TOTAL net adjustment from the prior is at most ±0.15, not ±0.15 per factor. Name the one or two strongest design features and stop. Arguments from mechanism, prior literature or population difficulty do not count as extra adjustments. Leave the prior's range only when the materials give numbers that settle the question arithmetically.
- Direction of primary endpoint favors intervention: about 0.65. Point estimates usually lean the intended way even when not significant. Single-arm diagnostic or device studies usually report in the hypothesized direction.
- Primary comparison reaches p<0.05: about 0.4. It is lower for small pilots, self-reported endpoints with heavy noise, or comparisons against historical controls. It is higher for large, well-powered trials with objective endpoints.
- Completion rate meets a threshold such as ≥80%: about 0.6. Completers are NOT the same as actual enrollment. A reached completion date says nothing about attrition. Long follow-up, many visits, or vulnerable populations lower the prior. Short single-visit device studies raise it, unless the convention divides by planned enrollment and actual enrollment fell short.
Example (invented): a 12-month trial with 10 visits and 90 enrolled out of 100 planned asks about ≥80% completion. It needs at least 80 completers, meaning 89% retention among those enrolled, over a long visit schedule. Answer: about 0.45.
