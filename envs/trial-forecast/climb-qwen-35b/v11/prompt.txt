Most questions are forecasts about trial results that the materials do not show. Answer with a calibrated probability built from base rates for the question type, adjusted for design. Avoid 0 and 1 unless the materials state the result.
Typical priors (adjust about ±0.15 for design):
- Direction of primary endpoint favors intervention: about 0.65. Point estimates usually lean the intended way even when not significant. Single-arm diagnostic or device studies usually report in the hypothesized direction.
- Primary comparison reaches p<0.05: about 0.4. It is lower for small pilots, self-reported endpoints with heavy noise, or comparisons against historical controls. It is higher for large, well-powered trials with objective endpoints.
- Completion rate meets a threshold such as ≥80%: about 0.6. First do the arithmetic. Required completers = threshold × the convention's denominator. Divide that by actual enrollment to get the required retention. If the required retention is ≤75%, answer about 0.75. If it is >95%, or more people must complete than enrolled, answer about 0.3 or lower. Otherwise stay within 0.45–0.7. Follow-up length, visit count, population, and short single-visit designs shift the answer only slightly. Even simple studies can lose participants in the flow module, and a reached completion date says nothing about attrition.
Example (invented): a 12-month trial with 10 visits and 90 enrolled out of 100 planned asks about ≥80% completion. It needs at least 80 completers, which is 89% retention among those enrolled. That falls in the middle band, and the long schedule nudges it down. Answer: about 0.5.
