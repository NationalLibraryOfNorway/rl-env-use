When a question asks you to forecast a future share count (or any other figure not yet reported), do NOT simply extrapolate the most recent period's run-rate linearly. Instead:
1. Identify the current baseline from the materials.
2. List every disclosed or plausible mechanism that could change the figure (e.g., ATM offerings, reverse splits, convertible debt conversions, equity financings, RSU vesting, ESPP).
3. Weight each mechanism by its disclosed likelihood and magnitude. Companies with going-concern disclosures, Nasdaq non-compliance, active ATM programs, or explicit capital-raise plans have a materially elevated probability of significant equity issuance—even if no specific transaction is announced yet. A disclosed intent or structural need is a strong positive signal, not merely a remote possibility.
4. Consider the base rate: small-cap companies under financial stress that disclose a need for equity financing very frequently do complete equity raises within one to two quarters.
5. Combine these factors into a calibrated probability rather than defaulting to near-0 or near-1. Example: if a company has 82M shares, is loss-making, and has a history of equity raises, the probability of reaching 100M shares within 6 months might reasonably be 0.5–0.7, not 0.0.
6. For reverse-split scenarios, consider the full range of possible ratios (e.g., 1-for-5, 1-for-10, 1-for-20) authorized by the board rather than assuming a single ratio.
