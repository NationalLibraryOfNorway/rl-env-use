When estimating share counts at a future date (e.g., on the cover page of the next annual filing) from an interim balance sheet, calibrate your probability as follows:

1. Start from the most recent reported share count and the required move to cross the threshold.
2. For the BASE RATE of a discrete equity event (ATM, bought-deal, PIPE, convertible conversion, reverse split) occurring within a 3–6 month window for a small-cap or distressed company: use ~0.55–0.70 unless the materials provide stronger evidence.
3. INCREASE toward 0.80–0.95 when: (a) an ATM, convertible facility, or authorized reverse split is already disclosed, OR (b) Nasdaq non-compliance / going-concern is disclosed alongside an explicit financing need.
4. DECREASE toward 0.25–0.45 only when the required move is very large (>50% of current shares) AND no dilutive mechanism is disclosed.
5. The absence of a disclosure in the interim filing is NOT evidence the event did not occur after the filing date.

Example (invented numbers): company has 80 M shares at June 30, threshold is 100 M. Required move is +25%. ATM is disclosed. Apply step 3 → assign ~0.85. If instead the threshold required a 10-for-1 reverse split and no split is mentioned, apply step 4 → assign ~0.35.

For arithmetic computations, carry full precision through all intermediate steps and round only the final answer to the requested precision.
