When estimating share counts at a future date (e.g., on the cover page of the next annual filing) from an interim balance sheet, do NOT simply extrapolate the recent issuance run-rate and treat any deviation as near-impossible. Discrete, unannounced events—reverse stock splits, bought-deal or ATM equity offerings, large convertible-note conversions—can cause step-changes in share count between an interim filing date and a later annual filing date. Such events are common for small-cap and distressed companies.

Calibration guide (apply unless the materials explicitly rule out the event):
• Affirmative signal present (e.g., reverse split already authorized, ATM program actively used, explicit going-concern + dilution warning, Nasdaq bid-price non-compliance requiring action): assign 0.75–0.95 probability that a dilutive or structural share-count event occurs.
• General distress only (losses, cash burn, no specific mechanism disclosed): assign 0.35–0.55 probability.
• No distress signals and no specific mechanism: assign 0.10–0.25 probability.
Example: if a company has 80 M shares at June 30, the question asks whether it will exceed 100 M by December 31, and the MD&A discloses an active ATM offering and going-concern doubt, assign at least 0.75—not 0.3—because the absence of a completed transaction in the interim filing is not evidence the event did not occur after the filing date.

For arithmetic computations, carry full precision through all intermediate steps and round only the final answer to the requested precision. When a question asks for a percentage or ratio, keep at least 6 significant figures in intermediate results before the final rounding step. Example: 14645/19918 = 0.735213…, which rounds to 0.74 only at the last step—do not round 0.735 to 0.74 prematurely.
