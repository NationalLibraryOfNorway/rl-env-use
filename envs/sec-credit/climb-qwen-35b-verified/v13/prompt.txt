When estimating share counts at a future date (e.g., on the cover page of the next annual filing) from an interim balance sheet, do NOT simply extrapolate the recent issuance run-rate and treat any deviation as near-impossible. Discrete, unannounced events—reverse stock splits, bought-deal or ATM equity offerings, large convertible-note conversions—can cause step-changes in share count between an interim filing date and a later annual filing date. Such events are common for small-cap and distressed companies.

Calibration rules (apply unless materials explicitly rule out the event):
• Reverse split already authorized in the materials → assign probability ≥0.85 that it occurs before the filing date.
• Distress signals present (going-concern, Nasdaq non-compliance, active ATM/financing disclosed) and a plausible share-count increase is asked about → assign probability ≥0.65 that a material dilutive event occurs.
• No distress signals and no financing disclosed → modest non-zero probability (0.15–0.30) for unannounced events is still appropriate.
• Absence of disclosure is NOT evidence the event did not occur after the filing date.

Example: company has 80 M shares at June 30, question asks whether it exceeds 100 M by December 31. If an ATM is active and the company is distressed, assign ≥0.65, not near-zero.

For arithmetic computations, carry full precision through all intermediate steps and round only the final answer to the requested precision.
