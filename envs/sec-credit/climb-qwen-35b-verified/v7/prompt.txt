When estimating share counts at a future date (e.g., on the cover page of the next annual filing) from an interim balance sheet, do NOT simply extrapolate the recent issuance run-rate and treat any deviation as near-impossible. Discrete, unannounced events—reverse stock splits, bought-deal or ATM equity offerings, large convertible-note conversions—can cause step-changes in share count between an interim filing date and a later annual filing date. Such events are common for small-cap and distressed companies.

Calibration guidance for share-count probability questions:
- If the materials explicitly authorize or disclose an imminent structural event (e.g., a reverse split has been authorized, an ATM program is active, convertible notes are outstanding with low conversion prices), assign probability ≥ 0.85 that the event occurs before the next filing date, unless the materials explicitly state it did not.
- If the company is distressed (going-concern, Nasdaq non-compliance, severe cash burn) AND the required share-count change is directionally consistent with a likely financing action (e.g., dilutive offering to raise cash), assign probability ≥ 0.80.
- If normal run-rate issuance alone nearly reaches the threshold (within ~15% of the target), assign probability ≥ 0.75 even without explicit event disclosure.
- Only assign probabilities below 0.5 when the materials provide positive evidence against the event (e.g., explicit statement that no offering occurred, or a reverse split would be needed but none is authorized).
- Absence of disclosure of an event in an interim filing is NOT evidence the event did not occur after the filing date.

Example: company has 80 M shares at June 30, authorized a reverse split, and Nasdaq non-compliance is disclosed. Question asks whether shares will be < 10 M by December 31. Assign probability ≈ 0.90, not 0.50.

For arithmetic computations, carry full precision through all intermediate steps and round only the final answer to the requested precision. When a question asks for a percentage or ratio, report the sign as specified by the question (e.g., if asked for 'net cash used' as a positive percentage of revenue, report a positive number).
