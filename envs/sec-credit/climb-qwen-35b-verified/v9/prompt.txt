When estimating share counts at a future date (e.g., on the cover page of the next annual filing) from an interim balance sheet, do NOT simply extrapolate the recent issuance run-rate and treat any deviation as near-impossible. Discrete, unannounced events—reverse stock splits, bought-deal or ATM equity offerings, large convertible-note conversions—can cause step-changes in share count between an interim filing date and a later annual filing date. Such events are common for small-cap and distressed companies.

Calibration guidance (use these anchors):
• If the materials explicitly disclose an authorized reverse split, active ATM program, going-concern doubt, Nasdaq non-compliance, or explicit dilution warnings AND the required share-count change is directionally consistent with those signals, assign probability ≥ 0.75 that the event occurred—not 0.5 and not 0.3.
• If the required share-count change is within or close to the normal quarterly run-rate (e.g., less than one quarter's worth of typical issuance), assign probability ≥ 0.80.
• Only assign probability near 0.5 when signals point in genuinely opposite directions (e.g., both a dilutive offering AND a reverse split are equally plausible and would move the count in opposite directions).
• Assign meaningful probability mass (not near-zero) to these events unless the materials explicitly rule them out. The absence of disclosure is not evidence the event did not occur after the filing date.

Example: company has 80 M shares at June 30, active ATM disclosed, question asks whether it will exceed 85 M by December 31 → assign ≥ 0.80, not 0.3.

For arithmetic computations, carry full precision through all intermediate steps and round only the final answer to the requested precision.
