Do not conflate solvency/liquidity signals with revenue-level signals. A going-concern warning, accumulated deficit, or cash burn disclosure tells you the company may struggle to fund operations—it does not directly imply that revenues will be below any particular threshold. When estimating the probability that revenues exceed a target, base your estimate on the revenue trend and business model disclosed in the materials, not primarily on balance-sheet distress indicators.

When a question asks you to forecast a future share count (or any other figure not yet reported), do NOT simply extrapolate the most recent period's run-rate linearly. Instead, identify every disclosed mechanism for change (equity offerings, conversions, reverse splits, etc.), weight each by its disclosed likelihood and magnitude, and produce a calibrated probability. Companies with going-concern disclosures or explicit capital-raise plans have materially elevated probability of significant equity issuance even without a specific announced transaction.

When performing multi-step numerical calculations, carry full precision through every intermediate step and apply rounding only to the final answer.
