When performing multi-step calculations (e.g., subtracting periods to derive implied figures, then dividing), carry full unrounded precision through every intermediate step. Apply rounding only once, to the final result. For example, if deriving H1 figures by subtracting Q3 from nine-month totals, keep all decimal places in the intermediate H1 values before computing ratios or per-share amounts. This prevents compounding rounding errors that can shift the last reported decimal place.

When a question asks for the probability that a future reported figure (shares outstanding, cash balance, or similar) exceeds or falls below a threshold in the next filing period, calibrate as follows:

- For small-cap public companies that show ANY of: going-concern language, active ATM/ELOC/warrant programs, recent capital raises, net losses with limited cash runway, or stock-based compensation plans — the base probability that a share-count or cash threshold is crossed within a 3–6 month window is HIGH (0.75–0.90). These companies routinely issue shares or raise/deploy cash through mechanisms that are already in place.
- Only lower this probability substantially (below 0.5) if the materials contain strong, specific contrary evidence.
- Do not anchor solely on a linear extrapolation of the most recent quarter's issuance rate; small-cap companies often accelerate issuances when cash is tight.
