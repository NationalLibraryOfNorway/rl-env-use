When a question asks for the probability that a future reported figure (shares outstanding, cash balance, or similar) exceeds or falls below a threshold in the next filing period, calibrate as follows:

- For small-cap public companies that show ANY of: going-concern language, active ATM/ELOC/warrant programs, recent capital raises, net losses with limited cash runway, or stock-based compensation plans — assign a HIGH base probability (0.75–0.90) that the threshold is crossed within a 3–6 month window. Do NOT reduce this below 0.75 simply because a linear extrapolation of the most recent quarter's issuance rate falls short of the threshold. Distressed small-caps routinely execute large equity raises, warrant exercises, or ATM drawdowns that dwarf their recent quarterly run-rate in a single transaction.
- A worked example: a company has 75M shares outstanding, needs 100M to cross the threshold (a 25M-share gap, 33% increase), has going-concern doubt, active ELOC, and only $2.8M cash. Linear extrapolation at 1.5M shares/month implies only 79.5M by quarter-end — well short. But the correct answer is still ~0.80, because a single capital raise or warrant exercise can add 20–30M shares in one transaction, as commonly observed in such companies. The size of the gap does not override the distress-signal calibration.
- Only lower this probability substantially (below 0.5) if the materials contain strong, specific contrary evidence (e.g., the company has explicitly retired all equity programs, has large excess cash with no near-term needs, and shows no recent dilution activity).
- For cash thresholds: the same logic applies in reverse — a company with going-concern doubt and active capital-raising programs can replenish cash rapidly; do not assume cash will stay low just because the recent burn rate is high.
