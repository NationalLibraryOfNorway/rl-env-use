When a question asks for the probability that a future reported figure (shares outstanding, cash balance, or similar) exceeds or falls below a threshold in the next filing period, calibrate as follows:

- For small-cap public companies that show ANY of: going-concern language, active ATM/ELOC/warrant programs, recent capital raises, net losses with limited cash runway, or stock-based compensation plans — the base probability that a share-count or cash threshold is crossed within a 3–6 month window is HIGH (0.75–0.90). These companies routinely issue shares or raise/deploy cash through mechanisms that are already in place.
- Only lower this probability substantially (below 0.5) if the materials contain strong, specific contrary evidence (e.g., the company has explicitly retired all equity programs, has large excess cash, and shows no dilution activity).
- CRITICAL: Do NOT anchor on whether a linear extrapolation of recent issuance rate reaches the threshold. Distressed small-caps almost always accelerate issuances when cash is tight, execute unannounced offerings, or draw equity lines beyond the recent pace. A worked example: a company has 21.8M shares, needs 24M to cross the threshold, has active SBC and financial stress but Q3 rate only adds ~0.8M/quarter → still assign 0.75+, because companies in distress routinely execute large equity raises or dramatically accelerate SBC in the very quarters when cash pressure peaks.
- The same logic applies to cash thresholds: a company burning cash rapidly with going-concern risk and active financing programs is highly likely to either raise new capital (pushing cash above a threshold) or fail to do so (pushing cash below), depending on the direction of the question — weight toward the outcome consistent with the distress signals.
- A worked example for shares: a company has 18M shares, needs 20M to cross the threshold, has an active ATM and going-concern doubt → assign ~0.80, not 0.05, because ATM drawdowns and stock compensation routinely add millions of shares per quarter in such companies.
